The button, six ways

The dial was drawn five ways before one was chosen. This is the same exercise for the thing next to it. There are already three buttons in the house that were never named as buttons — the mark, the pill, and a boxed one drawn five ways across four apps — and CHROME.md counts the cost: 63 button rules in font-proofer, 50 in ReCal, 16 in Kernpare, most of them that same box with its own padding and its own radius.

Two things get decided here, and only two. One enclosure — what a thing looks like when pressing it does something. One editable mark — what text looks like when it is yours to change. They are one decision because they are the same question asked twice, and because getting the second one wrong is how you end up with a bulleted list of unformatted links that each open a modal, with nothing on the page to say so. Every family below answers both in its own language, with the same content in the same card, so the comparison is between designs and not between demos.

What all six obey

cornersuperellipse(1.2), radius on the 2^k scale. Safari has no corner-shape and degrades silently to the plain radius — which is the corner every app shipped before the law, so nothing regresses.
target44px on a coarse pointer, bought as padding, never as height. A target has to exist; it does not have to be visible, and it must not be paid for by shrinking the mark.
padding-x≥ 0.6 × the radius. On a pill the radius is half the height, so a 34px pill owes 10.2px each side.
inkfaint / quiet / full = cannot act / present / chosen. Hover and pressed must not share an ink, or the ladder collapses.
markMaterial Symbols, one weight (300). Emphasis rides GRAD, so a row of marks cannot shift by a fraction as one lights up.
pressThe stroke lands before the fill: the stroke acknowledges the tap, the fill confirms on release.
focus2px, offset 2, on the signal. Never removed, in any family.

01

Keyline

A box, and one side of it. A hairline on a surface, and an editable line is the same box drawn with only its bottom edge — same radius, same inset, same colour — which completes as you approach. The law is one sentence: a closed keyline means press; a bottom-only keyline means change. It holds only if no rule under a heading is ever decorative.

Loud is not filled. The keyline thickens to 2.5px and takes full ink on both the edge and the label. It is still the same box — which is the point of the family.

02

Lozenge

A ground, not an edge. No hairline anywhere in the family. What it costs. Width. The padding rule is not optional on a pill and four icon buttons in a rail become a scroll. 03

Rule

The type is the control. No box in any state. An action is ink-full with a solid rule at 1px, thickening to the same two numbers the press law uses — 1.2 under the pointer, 1.8 held. An editable is the identical rule, dashed. Solid means it happens; dashed means it is yours. This is the one family that can be wrong in the exact way the brief names, and the condition is absolute: nothing else in the document may be underlined.

Screamer — the one call to action

No box at any size. The rule thickens with the type instead of staying 1px.

Enclosure

No icon-only form. There is nothing to draw it in. Those actions borrow 01.

The same rail

And this is where the family runs out. The rule scopes to the text, so in a row the leading mark, the label and the trailing mark are three objects at the same height with nothing holding them together — an underline cannot reach across a flex gap, and the row never becomes one thing. A box does that for free, which is the argument for a box. Widening the rule to span the whole row does bind it, and then it is a divider: the one mark this family has already means "press", so it cannot also mean "these belong together".

04

Plate

Hills and holes. Raised is press, recessed is type — and nobody has to be taught it. One lightness ramp does all of it: the plate is +L on the ground with the light from above, the well is −L with the light inside, and there is no hue and no second palette anywhere. Held, the plate goes into the ground rather than changing colour.

What it costs. The corner law already records the fault, and this family is built on the thing that has it: an inset box-shadow follows border-radius but not corner-shape, so every well and every held plate paints a rounded rectangle inside a superellipse, visible at the corners. That is not a bug to fix here — it is the engine — so choosing 04 means either keeping the radius small enough to hide it or giving up the house corner on exactly the controls that carry it most. It is also the only family that reads as a different decade.

05

Bracket

Corner ticks that close on approach. The box is there; only its corners are inked. --bt-tick is how far the ink runs along each edge, and hover animates it to 50% — at which point the four ticks meet and the enclosure completes. The ticks are a real border under two intersected masks, so they carry the G2 curve rather than imitating it. Editable text keeps only the left and right pair: brackets around the words, the way a field is marked on a drawing.

What it costs. mask-composite: intersect is the whole mechanism; where it is missing the mask collapses and the full border paints, which is family 01 — a correct button, so the degradation is honest, but a family whose fallback is another family is one design being maintained as two. At UI size the tick is 9px and four of them in a dense rail read as noise rather than as an edge. The transition needs --bt-tick registered with @property, or the ticks snap.

06

Rail

An enclosure means chosen, not pressable. The inversion of the five above, and the only one already running: this is font-proofer's rail. A row has no edge at rest — a mark, a label, and the full width of the column as its target — and the ground arrives when it is the one that is on. The editable mark is not a proposal either: it is .slider-number, the rule the dial ships. An underline drawn on the text, not a border drawn on the box — exactly as wide as the value, at the baseline rather than under the descender, and focus takes the digits and the rule together.

What it costs. A resting row states nothing on its own — which is the brief’s failure exactly, and the family survives it only on two conditions it cannot enforce for itself: every row carries a mark, and every row is the full width of its column. Strip either and this becomes the unformatted list. It also needs somewhere to be a rail: the row shape wants 200px and a left edge to sit against, so an action in the middle of a paragraph has to borrow another family. And because the enclosure is spent on chosen, there is nothing left to spend on primary — a destructive action here looks like every other row.

And it does not do prose. Visible in the situ card above: the rule is drawn on the text, so on a value it is four characters wide and on a wrapped sentence it is two full lines of underline. .slider-number was written for a number in a rail and it is right there. The editable case in the brief is a headline, not a value — so if 06 wins the rail, something else still has to win the paragraph, and the two have to look like one decision.

The screamer is not the UI button made bigger, and that is why it earns a row. The rail runs at opsz 10; Cal Sans's axis is 8 – 14 – 45 and a display line belongs at the top of it. Scaling a 13px button to 34px and leaving the optical size alone prints the small drawing large — thicker stems, looser fit — which is mis-drawn rather than merely big. Every screamer here runs opsz 45 and GEOM 50, which is what the real hero pill runs.

Weight is not part of “display”, though. The hero pill is also wght 700, and copying that to all six was a second emphasis stacked on whatever the enclosure was already doing — the rule icon.css states for the mark: once one thing carries the emphasis, a heavier stroke on top is two emphases stacked. A 2.5px keyline, a 0.1em rule, 3px ticks and a raised plate are each loud by themselves, so their type stays at 400, where Cal Sans is drawn. 02 keeps 700, and for a legibility reason rather than a volume one: it is the only screamer that inverts — dark ink on a signal field — and weight there is holding the counters open against a flat colour.

It reads tight on top because the bottom is carrying space nothing occupies. The face is left alone — no axis is touched. With line-height: 1 the baseline sits 0.8275em down, the cap top 0.1075em down, and the descender falls 0.0650em past the bottom of the line box; the hero pill then adds 0.38em of padding below against 0.22em above, which leaves 0.315em of clear air under the descender and a top gap that is mostly empty line box. text-box-trim removes that room rather than balancing it: trimmed to cap and baseline the box is the lettering itself, symmetric padding becomes optical centring by construction, and the descender hangs with 0.1025em to spare. The pill comes down from 1.70em to 1.40em and the type sits in the middle of it.

What none of the six demonstrate, and would have to be decided separately: a pending or loading state; a destructive action that has to look dangerous without a second hue; a label that wraps to two lines; a count or badge riding the enclosure; a split button; and an HDR chosen state on an enclosure rather than on a mark — the row below shows the mechanism, and no family assumes it.

One swatch, two ways to cut it

Not a seventh family — a mechanism, and one that already ships. Active is the only state that leaves SDR. The ladder spends everything getting to chosen — faint to quiet to full, GRAD 50 to 100 to 150 — and then has nowhere left to go on a screen that can go further. So the chosen mark is painted with a flat PQ white instead of with ink. src/icon.css does it to the mark; src/AxisSlider.css and src/AxisTriplet.css do it to the stepper chevron. font-proofer's own source carries none of it — it arrives through shared/ — so there is nothing here that is a port. The swatch below was read out of icon.css when this page was written, not retyped, and it is byte-identical in all three primitives.

The swatch

sRGB #fff
PQ

One pixel of field-247, its brightest — about 1216 nits through the PQ EOTF, and literally the same light the poster puts on its headline. Deliberately not PQ 1.0: that is 10,000 nits, which no display resolves, so the system tone-maps it — and clipping is where a bright white turns chalky and shifts hue. Sampling the field's own peak also means a mark glows at exactly the wordmark's white rather than outshining the logotype from 20px. If these two squares are the same colour, your screen is SDR and everything below will read as plain full ink.

Cut A — the glyph is the mask

color: transparent plus background-clip: text makes the engine rasterise the letterform as a mask rather than as smoothed coloured text. That is also why a chosen mark reads heavier than its GRAD implies — a mask is full grayscale coverage, more actual pixels, not bloom. Two layers make the boost a slope: ink on top at alpha (1 − hdr), HDR white underneath. At 0 the ink is opaque and the mark looks exactly as it did, which is why the handover is invisible. The paint itself cannot be interpolated, so only that one registered number animates.

Cut B — a shape is the mask

hold it

Same swatch, different cut: here it is the paint, and an SVG chevron masks it — so the thing that lights up is the thing you pressed, rather than a second shape appearing in a 10×6 box that already holds a chevron. Momentary is two durations, not an animation: opacity snaps on at :active with duration 0 and eases back out, so the flash cannot lag the press or be re-triggered mid-decay by a repeat from the hold timer. The mask is drawn at the chevron's hover stroke, because a press almost always arrives through a hover and a mask thinner than the stroke under it leaves a rim of ink around the light.

The light gate

chosen, on a light ground

On a light ground the whole thing is unwound: clip back to border-box so the glyph is coloured text again and not a mask, the number to 0, the range limit back to standard. Physics rather than taste — the boost exists only for pixels brighter than SDR white, and there is no darker-than-black to boost into. Left ungated, the swatch painted the chosen mark white on a light page: the faintest thing in the row instead of the fullest, which is the exact inverse of what active means.

What this does not settle. It is a technique for marks. Every family above decides a chosen state in ink, ground or edge, and none of them assumes a screen that goes past white. Putting an HDR chosen state on a button enclosure is a design question in its own right, not a port of this — so it is shown here and deliberately left out of all six.

Which one

One column matters more than the others. At rest is whether the thing announces itself with nobody pointing at anything — which is the property the brief asks for, and the one a hover state cannot supply.

Two of the six are already half-built. 06’s editable mark is not a candidate at all — it is .slider-number, shipping, in every dial in every app, and choosing anything else for editable text means the rail and the field disagree inside one panel. 01’s enclosure is src/button.css, likewise shipping. Those two fit together without being reconciled: 06 for the rail, where an enclosure means chosen and the row does the pointing, and 01 for everything outside it, where an enclosure means pressable. What that pairing has to survive is one panel holding both meanings of a box at once, which is a real risk and is the thing to test before it is adopted rather than after.

02 keeps the two jobs it already has — the one primary action, and one-of-N — as a variant, not a rival. 04 is the strongest at rest and is blocked by the engine, not by taste. 05 is the one worth keeping drawn for a hero or an empty state, where a single button can afford nine pixels of ceremony. 03 is the one to reject on purpose rather than by omission: it is the most typographic answer here and it fails exactly where the brief says not to.
