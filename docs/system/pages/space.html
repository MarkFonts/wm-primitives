The cap rule padding-x ≥ 0.6 × radius

A rounded end is shorter near the edge, so a letter placed there has less material above and below it than the padding number implies. Blue is the height that actually exists where the text starts. At corner-k 1.2 the shape reaches 95% of its height by 27px — 0.6r, marked by the ticks. The remaining 5% costs the other 17px, which is why the floor is not the full radius.

Use a step or change the design, not the number

The scale is a vocabulary, not a target to migrate toward. A value that is close to a step is the expensive kind of wrong: it reads as the scale to everyone who comes after, so it gets copied, and the copy is what drifts.

Do
  • Sit on a step. 4 · 6 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 48 · 64. Every second one doubles, so the scale stays legible when it is read as a series rather than a list.
  • Let steps compose. Nested padding adds up: a 4 inside a container plus an 8 on the row is the 12 the trigger already uses. Alignment falls out for free.
  • Spend the hairlines optically. 1, 2 and 3 are for a ring, a nudge, a bar — alignment, not spacing. They are outside the doubling on purpose.
  • Clear 0.6 × the radius on anything with a round cap, so the letters keep their full height where the shape is shortest.
Don’t
  • Don’t use a near-miss. 5, 7, 9, 10, 14, 20, 28, 40 and 54 all look like the scale at a glance and are not on it. Each is within 20% of a real step, which is exactly why nobody catches it.
  • Don’t tune a number to fix alignment. If two things do not line up, the sum of the nesting is wrong — a step is not.
  • Don’t pad a pill to satisfy the cap rule. Both live pills already clear it. The rule is a floor to check against, not a size to design to.
  • Don’t invent a step for one component. If nothing on the scale fits, the component is the thing to change.

The primitives and the instrument chrome already sit on the scale. Two places deliberately do not, and neither is an oversight: App.css is frozen, and the UI-kit board is a gallery of COSS components rather than house UI — nothing in it aligns against our chrome, so it keeps its own numbers.
