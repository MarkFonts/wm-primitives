The grid 24 columns, a 3px line

Twenty-four columns, a 3px line and one margin for every page — a standard the way InDesign has one, not a convention. Text beside text shares a first baseline, a two-line label meets its sample by its last line, a component moves whole. Everything on this page outside a stage is on the line, and on the columns too: the rail spans five of the 24, one stays empty, and the copy starts on the seventh and runs to the margin (below 1024 the rail is a bar and the copy takes all 12). A stage — a demo with type of its own, like the sheets below — keeps its own room, and is never half on the line. GRID.md is the standard in words; the bench is where it was cut.

Baseline 3px, the leading subdivided

The line is a unit every leading divides into, not a leading itself — the way 12/15 sits on a 3pt grid. Body is 16/24, eight units. Each block's first line is nudged down onto the next line, and every line after it is a whole number of units behind, so a long paragraph stays on the grid its first line was put on.

Body copy is where the grid is decided. At 24 every line of a long paragraph lands on a line. At 24.8 the first line still does — the page puts it there — and each line after it falls another 0.8px behind, until by the fourth it sits a full unit off and the paragraph beside it no longer shares a single line with this one. Only first lines are moved, and on purpose: a leading that is not whole units is a fault in the stylesheet.

Big type beside small

Set every lead in whole 3px lines, so a lead of 24, 27 or 48 is eight, nine or sixteen of them and a block's later lines stay on the grid its first line was put on.

In a row of side-by-side text, take the smallest lead as the row's step, and size the big text so its lead is a whole multiple of it — a size one token larger, never a loosened lead. Each headline line then lands on every other caption line.

Keep one step of white around big type, put rules and marks on the step, and when a row's leads cannot divide, change the size, not the grid. The full list is in GRID.md.

A row meets side by side, one baseline

Items that start in one row have their first line moved to the lowest first baseline among them. An item can meet the row by its last line instead, so a two-line caption ends on the big word's baseline rather than hanging below it. Only the block being aligned moves; the rest of each card keeps its own snap, so text set at the bottom of a card stays on its line. A shift larger than the text it moves is refused: that is a row that matched the wrong text.

Breakers and tools a page has a margin, a tool has none

A page keeps its margin; a tool has none, ever. wordmark.nyc and its Cal Sans case study are pages. font-proofer and ReCal are tools: their columns run edge to edge and the gutter stays, and a box that touches the window holds its text one gutter in from it, never flush. A breaker is the page's way out of its margin: a box that reaches the window's edge while its words stay at the margin. A picture breaker is all box.

A macOS window screenshot carries its drop shadow as a transparent margin, so the window sits inside the image and lands short of the columns. Flagged as a macOS shot, the shadow is cut out of the layout, and a file scaled after capture says by how much. Below, the window's edges sit on the lines of columns 11 and 24.
